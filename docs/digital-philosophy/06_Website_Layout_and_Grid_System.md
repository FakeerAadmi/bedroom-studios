# 06. WEBSITE LAYOUT AND GRID SYSTEM
**The 12-Column Architectural Matrix and Spatial Cadence**
*Version 1.0 — Autumn 2026*

---

## 1. THE GRID ARCHITECTURE

Bedroom Studios layouts are structured around an **asymmetric 12-column architectural matrix** inspired by Le Corbusier's *Modulor* and traditional Swiss publication grids:

```
GRID SPECIFICATIONS
├── Total Width: Max 1280px (Fluid with 32px – 64px outer gutters)
├── Desktop (≥ 1024px): 12 columns | 24px gutter
├── Tablet (768px – 1023px): 8 columns | 16px gutter
└── Mobile (≤ 767px): 4 columns | 16px gutter
```

### The Principle of Asymmetry
We reject rigid, symmetrical 3x3 photo grids where every box has the exact same dimensions. Instead, layouts juxtapose **wide expansive negative space against concentrated clusters of technical detail**:
- A large, dramatic 7-column hero photograph paired with a quiet 4-column textual column (1 column of breathing room between).
- Alternating cadences: Wide horizontal panorama → Two staggered vertical portrait studies → Full-width tectonic drawing.

---

## 2. NEGATIVE SPACE AS ARCHITECTURAL STILLNESS

In commercial e-commerce, white space is treated as "wasted inventory space" to be filled with upsells, badges, and carousels.

At Bedroom Studios, **negative space is an architectural material**:
- It represents the quiet, empty air of a room that allows an object to breathe.
- Vertical section spacing is deliberate and generous: `120px` to `160px` between primary narrative blocks on desktop (`64px` to `80px` on mobile).
- The user's eye must never feel rushed or suffocated.

---

## 3. CROPPING, OVERLAPPING, AND HORIZON PLANES

### 1. The Horizon Plane
Whenever objects are presented in a collection or shelf context, their bases must align to a single, continuous **horizon baseline**. This anchors them to the same gravitational floor.

### 2. Strategic Overlapping
To establish three-dimensional spatial depth, typography and objects may selectively overlap:
- A large, delicate serif headline set in low-opacity ink (`rgba(28,28,26,0.06)`) sitting *behind* the silhouette of a cast concrete lamp.
- This creates the visual effect of an object standing in front of an architectural wall.

### 3. Edge-Bleed vs. Framed Boundaries
- **Environment & Atmospheric Shots (Mode B)**: May bleed edge-to-edge across the full viewport to immerse the visitor in the physical room.
- **Tectonic Product Studies (Mode A & C)**: Are framed within disciplined grid columns, anchored by fine 1px guidelines that feel like technical draughtsman drawings.
