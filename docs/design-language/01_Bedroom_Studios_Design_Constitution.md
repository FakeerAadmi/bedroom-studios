# 01. BEDROOM STUDIOS — DESIGN CONSTITUTION
**The Master Code for Objects, Spaces, and Systems**
*Version 1.0 — Autumn 2026*

---

## PREAMBLE
Bedroom Studios is an independent Indian design studio. We reject both the disposable novelty of hobbyist digital fabrication and the hollow nostalgia of heritage tourist crafts. We operate at the intersection of architectural discipline, contemporary digital manufacturing, and heavy mineral materiality.

We do not make "decor." We make **considered desktop instruments and architectural luminaires** designed to ground everyday spaces in stillness, focus, and tactile presence.

Every object carrying our mark must obey this Constitution without compromise.

---

## THE TEN LAWS OF BEDROOM STUDIOS DESIGN

### LAW 1: MATERIAL HONESTY OVER SURFACE ILLUSION
Never mimic another material. If an object is 3D-printed in polymer, celebrate its additive toolpaths, layer micro-steps, and optical diffusion properties; never sand and spray-paint it to look like injection-moulded ABS or faux metal. If an object is cast cement, let its mineral aggregates, pinhole air vents, and structural weight be felt. Materials must be authentic to their molecular nature.

### LAW 2: THE GRAVITY MANDATE (GROUNDED PRESENCE)
Every desktop object must possess deliberate physical mass. We despise lightweight objects that skid, tip over, or feel ephemeral when touched. If a luminaire has a lightweight printed shade, it must be counterbalanced by a solid mineral plinth or turned bronze core. An object must declare its presence through gravitational stability.

### LAW 3: ARCHITECTURAL GEOMETRY, NEVER ARBITRARY ORGANICISM
Forms are derived from structural, optical, or spatial logic. We favour stepped fluting, pure cylinders, chamfered monoliths, tectonic cantilevers, and crisp planar intersections. Curves must have geometric intent (catenary arcs, parabolic reflectors, circular fillets), never arbitrary blobby organicism born of freehand digital sculpting.

### LAW 4: THE INTEGRATED JUNCTION
How two materials meet defines our craft. A polymer shade must never be glued sloppily to a cement base. Junctions must be intentional: brass press-fit collars, keyed interlocks, stepped recesses, or mechanical fasteners. The joint is not hidden—it is celebrated as an architectural detail.

### LAW 5: TACTILE REWARD
Every product must reward physical contact. Switches must click with mechanical snap; surfaces must transition between cool mineral stone, warm satin polymer, and cold brushed brass; bases must glide silently on high-density wool felt. If an object feels disappointing to pick up, it is a failed design.

### LAW 6: CALIBRATED ILLUMINATION, NOT BARE DIODES
Light is a physical material. We never expose raw LED chips or harsh direct glare. Light must always pass through a mediating architecture: ribbed diffusion baffles, washi-textured walls, indirect bounce domes, or stained-glass jewel filters. We build instruments for *godhuli* (dusk) and late-night solitary focus—warm (2400K–2700K), soft, and directional.

### LAW 7: SILENT SERVICE OVER VISUAL SHOUTING
Our objects live in spaces where people read, write, build, code, and think. They must not scream for attention on social media feeds at the expense of quiet domestic harmony. An object should look at peace sitting unlit on a dark walnut shelf for twelve hours a day. Restraint is the highest declaration of confidence.

### LAW 8: SUBTLE CULTURAL ROOTS, ZERO EXOTIC CLICHÉ
We are rooted in contemporary India—the brutalist concrete of Chandigarh and Ahmedabad, the raw brass of Moradabad, the timber joinery of Bengaluru, and the evening ritual of lighting incense and lamps. We reject all tourist clichés: no paisley, no painted elephants, no faux-royal filigree. Our Indian identity is expressed through material density, spatial ritual, and architectural honesty.

### LAW 9: REPAIRABILITY AND SECURE MODULARITY
Electronics, light engines, and fasteners must be accessible. Never weld or chemically fuse electronics into unopenable sealed blocks. A customer should be able to service an LED core, replace a USB-C board, or renew a pull-cord after a decade of daily use.

### LAW 10: ZERO COMPROMISE FOR "EASE OF PRINTING"
We do not design objects simply because our 3D printers can print them overnight without supports. Manufacturing serves the design; design never surrenders to the limitations of hobbyist CAD. If a geometry requires five toolings, multi-axis machining, or a two-part silicone mould pour, we do the work.
