## 1. THE SENSORY BENCHMARK

Most consumer electronics and modern desk objects are designed purely for the eyes—rendered in frictionless CAD software and evaluated on flat screens. When picked up, they disappoint: they feel light, plasticky, cold, and hollow.

At Bedroom Studios, **touch is the primary sense**. We design from the palm outward.

---

## 2. HOW A BEDROOM STUDIOS OBJECT FEELS IN THE HAND

When you pick up an object crafted by Bedroom Studios, your nervous system receives three immediate confirmations of quality:

### 1. Surprising, Reassuring Density
The object is heavier than your visual expectation. An incense plinth that looks like a small stepped cylinder weighs 750 grams. A desktop task lamp weighs 1.2 kilograms. The mass conveys geological permanence—it cannot be casually bumped out of place by an elbow or cable yank.

### 2. Micro-Tactile Tooth (Mineral Aggregate)
The surface of our cast cement is not glassy, polished, or slippery. It has a dry, fine-grained tooth—similar to honed slate or river-tumbled basalt. You feel the microscopic quartz aggregates and gentle thermal conductivity of stone.

### 3. Edge Articulation (Tactile Gripping Surfaces)
Every surface transition features a deliberate physical register. Vertical flutes provide natural ridges for fingers to nest into when lifting. Chamfered 45° baselines provide a continuous undercut that allows fingers to easily slip underneath an object to pick it up off a flat walnut desk without sliding it.

## 3. HOW A BEDROOM STUDIOS OBJECT REWARDS INTERACTION

Every interaction must be an acoustic, kinetic, and physical event that punctures the monotony of digital work:

### The Pull-Chain Switch
- **Kinetic Travel**: 18mm to 24mm of physical downward stroke.
- **Tension**: Provided by an internal 1.2N stainless steel return spring that requires deliberate human effort to pull.
- **Acoustic Signature**: A dual-stage metallic click:
  - *Primary Click* (pull): Deep, resonant metallic latch at 1250Hz.
  - *Secondary Snap* (release): Crisp brass ball return at 1850Hz.
- **Pendulum Motion**: Once released, the brass teardrop fob oscillates with naturally decaying pendulum swings for 3 to 4 seconds, bringing subtle kinetic life into the room.

### The Rotary Potentiometer Dimmer
- **Damping**: Fluid-filled rotary mechanism using high-viscosity silicone fluorosilicone damping grease.
- **Resistance**: Consistent, velvety resistance across 300 degrees of rotation.
- **Knob**: Solid turned C360 brass with 0.8mm diamond knurling that grips the fingertips without scratching.
- **Detent**: A positive mechanical "off" click at the zero point so the user knows without looking that the circuit is physically broken.

### The Landing (The Felt Plinth)
- When a Bedroom Studios object is placed onto a glass desk, marble counter, or solid oak table, it must never emit a sharp, jarring clatter.
- Every base is lined with a precision laser-cut **1.5mm high-density German wool felt pad** (color: Charcoal Melange).
- The acoustic landing is a muted, deep, satisfying **"thud"** that protects both the furniture surface and the object.

## 4. TACTILE CHECKLIST FOR PROTOTYPES

Before approving a production run, every prototype is tested against the **Physical Contact Protocol**:

1. **The Blindfold Test**: Close your eyes and run your fingers across every seam. Can you feel any sharp flash, glue seep, or ragged burrs? (If yes, reject).
2. **The One-Handed Lift**: Can the object be lifted cleanly with one hand without slipping, even if hands are dry? (If it slips, add fluting or base chamfer).
3. **The Cable Tug Test**: Plug a braided USB-C cable into the object and tug it with 200g of lateral force. Does the base skid or tip? (If it moves, increase base ballast).
4. **The Acoustic Decay**: Does the switch or moving part sound like plastic clicking or metal latching? (Must register as deep, damped mechanical audio).
