# 03. PRODUCT DESIGN LANGUAGE
**Core Design Principles & Operational Rules for Object Creation**
*Version 1.0 — Autumn 2026*

---

## OVERVIEW
These principles are not marketing slogans or aspirational mood statements. They are **binding physical criteria** used by designers and makers at Bedroom Studios to develop, critique, and approve every physical product before tooling or batch production.

---

## PRINCIPLE 1: THE GRAVITATIONAL ANCHOR (MASS DISTRIBUTION)
### 1. Explanation
Every object must have a low center of gravity and sufficient mass to ensure it cannot be accidentally knocked, slid, or tilted during normal desk activity.
### 2. Why It Matters
A lightweight object communicates provisional, cheap manufacturing. Heavy objects anchor human trust and confer spatial permanence.
### 3. In Practice
70% or more of an object's total mass resides in the lower third of its vertical volume. Felt-padded footprints provide high-friction damping.
### 4. For the Designer
Never design a tall, top-heavy silhouette without an engineered ballast plinth in the base (cement cast or turned steel core). Minimum desk object mass is 400g; luminaires must exceed 850g.
### 5. Violations
Hollow plastic shells that skid when plugging in a USB cable or pulling a switch.
### 6. In a Lamp
The BOOFA lamp: An ultra-lightweight fluted top dome paired with a flared, solid-cast cement lower column weighing 1100g.
### 7. In Cementware
The Step Incense Pedestal: 750g stepped solid Portland base with a recessed brass cup that does not wobble even when bumped.
### 8. In a Future Product
A headphone stand: Solid cast concrete brutalist foot with a slim cantilevered brass suspension arm.

## PRINCIPLE 2: TECTONIC DUALITY (LIGHT ON HEAVY)
### 1. Explanation
Contrast is achieved not through surface paint, but by combining an ethereal, light-diffusing upper element with a dense, grounded mineral foundation.
### 2. Why It Matters
It creates dynamic visual tension—light floating above stone, digital precision resting on geological mass.
### 3. In Practice
Translucent additive-manufactured polymers or stained glass sitting upon high-density cast concrete or dark oiled walnut.
### 4. For the Designer
Always ask: *What is grounding this object, and what is breathing light or air into it?* Never make an all-plastic assembly when a mineral counterweight can be introduced.
### 5. Violations
Monochromatic, single-material plastic injection mouldings that look like bath toys.
### 6. In a Lamp
Modern Shoji Lamp: Delicately layered washi-textured polymer diffusion louvres locked into a dark cast cement perimeter collar.
### 7. In Cementware
Desk Organizer Tray: Heavy charcoal concrete exterior cradle containing removable machined raw brass coin dishes.
### 8. In a Future Product
Desktop Sound Monitor / Speaker: Cast stone acoustic enclosure with a fine laser-perforated brass or woven polymer grille.

## PRINCIPLE 3: CELEBRATION OF THE TOOLPATH (MICRO-TEXTURE)
### 1. Explanation
Honor the manufacturing process. The 0.16mm layer stratification of additive manufacturing and the tiny air voids of cast aggregate are legitimate surface textures, not defects to be hidden.
### 2. Why It Matters
Surface authenticity separates craft from mass-extruded synthetic junk. Attempts to make 3D prints look like injection-moulded plastic always fail and look cheap.
### 3. In Practice
Specially oriented slicing parameters (vase mode, gyroid infill translucency, 45-degree micro-ribbing) that refract light softly.
### 4. For the Designer
Design features that accentuate layer orientation. Ensure layer lines run parallel to the primary light travel path to eliminate harsh internal hot-spots.
### 5. Violations
Thick, glossy spray paint, Bondo filler, or sanding down layers to create a dead, fake plastic gloss.
### 6. In a Lamp
Hot Air Balloon Lamp: Ribbed diffusion envelope with micro-stepped vase-mode ridges that create a moiré diffraction pattern when lit.
### 7. In Cementware
Square Plinth: Hand-poured with controlled entrapped air bubbles along the chamfered corners, sealed with matte penetrating siloxane.
### 8. In a Future Product
Wall Sconce: Continuous spiral-extruded polymer fluted shade that casts geometric ribbed shadow ladders across plaster walls.

## PRINCIPLE 4: THE HONEST MECHANICAL TRANSITION (NO GLUE)
### 1. Explanation
Parts must join through mechanical logic—threaded fasteners, press-fit brass bushings, keyed slots, or gravity registers—never permanent chemical adhesives.
### 2. Why It Matters
Glue fails over time, prevents disassembly, prohibits recycling, and denies the user the pleasure of servicing their object.
### 3. In Practice
Visible countersunk hex-drive M3 brass screws, threaded knurled couplings, and slip-fit silicone dampeners.
### 4. For the Designer
Design every joint to be disassembled in under three minutes with a standard 2.5mm hex key or screwdriver.
### 5. Violations
Hot-glue guns, cyanoacrylate seam welds, or snap-fit tabs that snap off and ruin the product permanently.
### 6. In a Lamp
Pull-chain switch socket mechanically threaded into a solid bronze neck collar with a concealed M4 set-screw.
### 7. In Cementware
Incense brass cup: Turned solid brass insert friction-fit into a silicone gasket seated inside the cured concrete cavity.
### 8. In a Future Product
Modular Desk Shelf: Slotted walnut risers interlocked with cast aluminum bracket feet using exposed knurled thumbscrews.

## PRINCIPLE 5: THE CALIBRATED SPECTRUM (WARMTH AS MATERIAL)
### 1. Explanation
Light is treated with the same physical rigor as concrete or timber. Color temperature is strictly clamped between 2200K (candle warmth) and 2700K (incandescent dusk).
### 2. Why It Matters
Cold blue light (4000K–6500K) spikes cortisol, produces eye strain, and destroys the contemplative intimacy of evening work.
### 3. In Practice
High CRI (≥95), flicker-free 5V or 12V LED cores combined with amber/cream diffusion matrices.
### 4. For the Designer
Never specify an off-the-shelf cool white diode. Calculate the wall thickness of the diffuser to ensure the output chromacity remains warm and golden.
### 5. Violations
Harsh 6000K blue-white LEDs, pulsing PWM drivers, RGB rainbow cycle modes, or visible bare diode points.
### 6. In a Lamp
The Tiffany Hero Lamp: Dual-layer diffusion ensuring internal 2400K incandescent bulb blooms through jewel glass without glare.
### 7. In Cementware
Candle Holder Plinth: Sized to house 38mm tealights with flame height calibrated to reflect off a polished brass rear disc.
### 8. In a Future Product
Ambient Horizon Bar: A solid concrete linear tube with a concealed downward-facing 2200K light engine illuminating desk work.

## PRINCIPLE 6: ARCHITECTURAL ORDER (CHAMFERS & REVEALS)
### 1. Explanation
Edges and transitions are governed by deliberate architectural details: 45° micro-chamfers (1.5mm–3mm) and 2mm shadow reveals.
### 2. Why It Matters
Flush, zero-tolerance meetings between two materials always look misaligned due to manufacturing tolerances. A shadow reveal makes the junction intentional.
### 3. In Practice
Where a polymer housing meets a cement base, a 2mm deep by 1.5mm wide shadow gap separates them, framing each material cleanly.
### 4. For the Designer
Never draw a razor-sharp 90° raw edge in CAD; always apply a 1.2mm–2.5mm chamfer or fillet. Chamfers catch light and prevent chipping.
### 5. Violations
Razor-sharp raw edges that catch skin or chip, or sloppy flush joints where one material overhangs the other by 0.5mm.
### 6. In a Lamp
Base-to-stem collar with a 2mm recessed shadow line that prevents cement dust transfer and hides internal cable relief.
### 7. In Cementware
Brutalist Desk Tray: Stepped 45-degree chamfers around the outer perimeter that catch directional desktop task light.
### 8. In a Future Product
Pen Cradle: Machined brass cylinder with 45-degree end chamfers resting in a V-grooved fluted concrete pedestal.

## PRINCIPLE 7: ACOUSTIC AND KINETIC SATISFACTION
### 1. Explanation
Every moving part must sound and feel solid, deliberate, and damped.
### 2. Why It Matters
Sound is tactile confirmation of quality. A tinny click feels disposable; a deep, damped metallic latch feels enduring.
### 3. In Practice
Brass beaded chains with counterweighted teardrop fobs; rotary potentiometers with hydraulic silicone damping; heavy felt feet that land with a muted thud.
### 4. For the Designer
Test the acoustic signature of switches and moving joints. Avoid lightweight micro-switches that emit high-pitch clicks.
### 5. Violations
Wobbly rocker switches, flimsy touch sensors that trigger accidentally, or lightweight hollow parts that rattle.
### 6. In a Lamp
Weighted brass teardrop pull-chain that swings with natural pendulum damping and trips an internal 1.2N mechanical toggle switch.
### 7. In Cementware
Concrete coaster set: Each coaster features a 1.5mm high-density wool felt base that sets down on oak or glass without clatter.
### 8. In a Future Product
Rotary Dimmer Console: Heavy solid brass knurled knob turning on a 250kΩ fluid-damped potentiometer with silky resistance.

---

## PRINCIPLE 8: THE NOBLE AGEING MANDATE (PATINA COMPATIBILITY)
### 1. Explanation
Every material chosen must improve with age or maintain its integrity over decades.
### 2. Why It Matters
Objects that look worse the moment they leave the box are consumerist waste. Objects that gather character become heirlooms.
### 3. In Practice
Raw unlacquered brass that oxidizes into rich deep ochres; high-density mineral cement that deepens with handling oils; UV-stabilized PETG that does not yellow.
### 4. For the Designer
Never apply clear lacquer coats over brass that crack and peel. Allow the metal to breathe and oxidize naturally.
### 5. Violations
Rubberized "soft-touch" coatings that degrade into sticky sludge after three years; cheap electroplating that flakes off.
### 6. In a Lamp
Solid brass pull-chain and switch finial that absorb natural finger oils, creating a dark burnished patina over five years of daily use.
### 7. In Cementware
Incense Ash Vessel: Concrete sealed with natural carnauba wax; incense smoke oils create a subtle, beautiful dark bloom over time.
### 8. In a Future Product
Desk Mat Weight / Paperweight: Solid cast bronze cube with raw tumbled corners that polish down where the hand touches it most.
