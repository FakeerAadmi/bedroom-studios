## 2. HOW FAMILIES CONNECT WITHOUT BECOMING CLONES

To prevent the studio from becoming repetitive while maintaining absolute brand coherence, every object across all families shares **Four Universal Anchor Details**:

### 1. The 45° Base Undercut
Every object in Family I, II, III, and IV features a 45° chamfered undercut along its lowest contact perimeter. This lifts the object 2mm off the desk visually, creating a delicate floating shadow line while allowing fingertips to grip underneath.

### 2. The Recessed C360 Brass Collar
Whenever metal is introduced (whether holding a switch, seating an incense stick, or framing an organizer tray), the metal is **unlacquered, 240-grit brushed C360 brass**, recessed by exactly 1.5mm to 2.0mm into the host concrete or walnut.

### 3. The Fluting Metric
Vertical flutes across the studio follow a consistent geometric rhythm:
- Small objects (incense pedestals, pen wells): **6mm fluting pitch, 3mm depth**.
- Large objects (lamp bases, large bowls): **14mm fluting pitch, 6mm depth**.
When an incense pedestal sits next to a BOOFA lamp, the fluting speaks the same mathematical language.

### 4. The Thermal Footprint
All desk-surface objects are balanced to feature the same tactile base: dark charcoal aggregate concrete grounded by laser-cut German wool felt.

## 3. HOW A NEW PRODUCT IS BORN

A new product idea is only accepted into the family system if it passes the **Typology Cross-Examination**:

1. *Which family does it ground?* (Must fit into one of the five families).
2. *What is its daily ritual?* (Must have a clear human interaction: illuminated at dusk, touched when writing, lit during incense meditation).
3. *What is its acoustic and gravitational signature?* (Must meet the minimum mass and damping requirements).
4. *Can it sit on the walnut display shelf beside the Tiffany lamp without clashing?* (Must harmonize with the studio's chromatic and material universe).
