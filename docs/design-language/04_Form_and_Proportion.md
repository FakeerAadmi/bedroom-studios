# 04. FORM AND PROPORTION
**The Geometric Grammar of Bedroom Studios Objects**
*Version 1.0 — Autumn 2026*

---

## 1. PREFERRED GEOMETRIES

Bedroom Studios draws its formal lineage from **brutalist architecture, industrial machinery, and optical physics**. We do not design arbitrary organic shapes. Every curve and line is anchored to Euclidean or structural logic.

### Primary Geometric Archetypes
1. **The Monolith (Extruded Polyhedron)**: Square and rectangular prisms with controlled bevels. Used for heavy plinths, pedestals, and structural trays.
2. **The Fluted Column (Vertical Rhythms)**: Cylinders and cones relieved by vertical semicircular or trapezoidal flutes. Flutes serve two functional goals: they increase rigid surface area for thin-wall additive prints and break direct light into soft gradient bands.
3. **The Stepped Ziggurat (Terraced Mass)**: Successive geometric steps that taper mass upward while keeping weight centered at the footprint.
4. **The Catenary Arch & Dome**: Parabolic and catenary curves that resolve downward compressive forces naturally and reflect light without harsh interior focal hot-spots.

## 2. CORNERS, RADII, AND EDGES

### The 1.5mm–3mm Rule
- **No razor-sharp 90° corners**: A raw 90° edge in concrete will chip; in 3D-printed polymer, it creates stress concentrations and feels aggressive to touch.
- **The Chamfer Preference**: We prefer a **45° flat chamfer** (1.5mm on small objects, 3mm on larger plinths) over a rounded fillet on external corners. Chamfers catch light crisply and give concrete the feel of cut ashlar stone.
- **The Internal Fillet Mandate**: Internal corners must always have a **minimum 2.0mm radius** to ensure clean mould release for cement and structural strength for printed layer bonds.

## 3. PROPORTION & VISUAL WEIGHT

### The 1:2.6 Golden Ratio of Mass
When combining a base and a luminaire/diffuser:
- **Base Volume to Diffuser Volume**: The base should visually occupy between 25% and 35% of the total height, but account for **at least 65% of the physical weight**.
- This creates the visual effect of an object firmly rooted to the earth, supporting an ethereal illuminated volume above it.

### Wall Thickness Standards
- **3D-Printed Diffusion Walls**: Exactly **1.2mm to 1.6mm** (3 to 4 perimeters with a 0.4mm nozzle). Thinner creates fragility and visible infill shadows; thicker chokes the light output and creates muddy opacity.
- **Cast Cement Walls**: Minimum **8.0mm to 12.0mm**. Anything thinner than 8mm in Portland aggregate risks micro-cracking during de-moulding.
- **Solid Brass Components**: Minimum **2.5mm to 4.0mm** for structural rings and turnings. Never use thin stamped sheet brass (0.5mm) that flexes or rings like tin.

---

## 4. OPENINGS AND NEGATIVE SPACE

### The Shadow Reveal (The 2mm Gap)
Whenever two disparate materials meet (e.g. concrete base and polymer shade), they must **never sit flush**. A flush joint emphasizes microscopic manufacturing tolerances. Instead, create a **2.0mm deep by 1.5mm wide shadow reveal**. The shadow reveal frames the transition and lets each material stand on its own integrity.

### Cable & Port Integration
- **USB-C Ports**: Must be recessed by 2.5mm within an architectural slot with a 45° chamfered lead-in for easy tactile alignment in the dark.
- **Cable Exits**: Must exit the base at the lowest possible center plane, through a radiused notch lined with a silicone grommet to prevent friction wear.

## 5. SIGNATURE MOVES

A signature move is a recurring formal detail that identifies a Bedroom Studios object without a logo. We employ two genuine, structurally motivated signature moves:

### Signature Move 1: The Stepped Fluting (The "Architectural Stepped Ridge")
A vertical fluted surface that terminates in a stepped terrace. Seen on the *Step Incense Pedestal* and the *BOOFA Lamp base*. It provides tactile grip for picking up heavy stone objects and creates dramatic high-contrast light and shadow ridges under desk lamps.

### Signature Move 2: The Machined Brass Reveal Collar
A turned brass ring positioned precisely at the mechanical junction between stone and polymer. It serves three real functions:
1. It acts as the mechanical threaded clamp holding the assembly together.
2. It acts as an internal thermal heat-sink for the LED engine.
3. Visually, it introduces a warm metallic horizon between cold stone and warm illuminated diffusion.

---

## 6. WHAT WE EXPLICITLY AVOID IN FORM
- **Freehand "Digital Clay" Sculpting**: Blobby organic forms with no geometric center or radius.
- **Decorative Non-Functional Fins**: Airplane-style fins or decorative ridges added purely to look "futuristic."
- **Compound Complex Splines**: Shapes that cannot be dimensioned or checked with standard architectural orthographic projections.
- **Faux-Handmade Irregularities**: Digitally introducing random noise into CAD models to fake a hand-thrown ceramic aesthetic.
